Abstract

Semantic models recognize what a 3D scene contains, but not what it is made of. Hyperspectral imaging provides material evidence, yet costly acquisition, missing composition annotations and expensive multi-view unmixing hinder its use at scene scale. We present Spectral World Renderer, in which a coding agent turns reference images and text into executable scene programs; measured spectra and shared rendering samples then yield aligned hyperspectral observations, material labels and sub-pixel proportions, so that a 3D decomposition can be verified. The resulting SWR-Bench comprises eight indoor and outdoor worlds with material and illumination variation. Abundance-GS decodes mixtures over a shared endmember dictionary at every ray intersection of a compact Gaussian representation, so every primitive carries queryable abundances, and a multi-view appearance regularization assigns endmembers to distinct appearances without material labels. It leads or ties identification in all eight worlds, has the best mean PSNR on real captures, trains 3.7–12.8× faster than unmixing baselines and separates RGB metamers.

Application

Seeing beyond RGB

Living leaves differ from materials of the same colour beyond the visible range: chlorophyll absorbs red light and leaf tissue reflects near-infrared light, producing a red edge near 700 nm that minerals and dry leaves lack. In City, foliage-coloured mineral leaves are only 2° from foliage in RGB but 42° apart in spectrum, so they receive endmembers of their own.

Because abundances are stored on explicit, rasterizable Gaussians, a material query is a selection over primitives that is consistent across views and requires neither labels nor a per-image classifier. The mineral-leaf query retrieves no ordinary tree, whereas the equivalent RGB query retrieves eleven.

How it works · Data

Worlds whose composition is known by construction

Real multi-view hyperspectral datasets are tabletop or turntable captures without material labels or sub-pixel proportions, which cannot be annotated reliably by hand. Spectral World Renderer therefore generates worlds rather than annotating captures.

1

Generate the world

A coding agent turns reference images and text into an executable scene program that fixes geometry, materials, illumination and cameras.

2

Ground it in measurements

Every material is assigned measured spectra, with specimen variation and changes in spectral shape between sunlight and shade.

3

Render aligned evidence

Shared sub-pixel samples render RGB and hyperspectral observations together with material labels and area proportions.

How it works · Representation

Abundance-GS: abundances at every ray intersection

Spectral radiance fields reconstruct spectra without decomposing them, and unmixing rendered spectra afterwards cannot separate what compositing has already mixed. Abundance-GS resolves the mixture before compositing.

A

Band-independent storage

An RGB-trained anchor provides compact geometry, and each Gaussian stores a feature instead of a spectrum, so storage does not grow with the number of bands.

B

Decode before compositing

A shared decoder maps the feature at every ray intersection to abundances over a scene-wide endmember dictionary, so every Gaussian carries queryable abundances.

C

Separate intensity and appearance regularization

A separate intensity absorbs shading, and a multi-view appearance regularization assigns an over-complete set of endmembers to distinct appearances.

Multi-view appearance regularization

Keeping similar materials apart without labels

Because the intensity absorbs brightness, a single endmember can explain two spectrally similar materials: in Forbidden City, white marble is four times brighter than grey masonry but only 3.8° apart in spectral shape.

Clustering the pixels of all training views by spectral shape and brightness separates such appearances, and every view shares the same clusters. Each cluster initializes its own endmember, and the assignment is retained as a weak regularizer during training, which gives marble its own layer.

Results

Fidelity and identification, measured separately

Abundance-GS identifies materials best or on par in every world and learns the endmembers closest to the true spectra. A lower spectral angle does not imply better identification: in Market and Study, 3DGS with post-hoc unmixing reaches a lower SAM but a far lower mIoU.

Analysis

What the representation learns

The decoded quantities can be compared directly with the stored ground truth, and the abundances that a query selects are attributes of individual Gaussians.

  • Within one specimen, the logarithm of the composited intensity correlates with that of the true radiance (r = 0.97 on Market, 0.87 on City).
  • The sun or shadow state explains 84 % and 67 % of the within-class variance of the intensity, but only 3 % and 15 % of the variance of the class mass.
  • Abundances change little with the viewing direction on the Lambertian worlds (total variation at most 0.04 within 45°).

Primitives as material carriers

Each Gaussian is enclosed by the regular tetrahedron of its canonical frame, scaled, rotated and translated with the Gaussian. The clips dissolve from these bounding tetrahedra into the rendered view along the camera path of the material queries above.
